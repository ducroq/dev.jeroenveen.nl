## The claim

Crafting prompts for systems like vmodel and ovr produces a distinct feeling: you say the right words in the right order and the system returns something clean, fluent, authoritative. The hit of "I cast it correctly and it obeyed." That feeling is a real craft signal, but what it tracks is fluency, not correctness. A spell's whole premise is that correct incantation guarantees effect. A prompt's output is plausible whether or not it is right, and the more fluent it is the harder the spell-feeling fires. So the feeling is strongest exactly when you are least protected. It is a signal to validate, not to trust. It marks the moment ground truth is missing, not the moment it arrived.

## Spine (one move, held the whole way)

1. **The scene.** Crafting prompts for vmodel and ovr. The moment one clicks and the output comes back clean and authoritative. The small hit of having said the right words. Grounded, specific, my own work. First sentence filters for the Section-2 reader.
2. **Name the feeling honestly.** It is a real craft signal. You are getting better at this. The feeling is not fake, and the essay does not sneer at it.
3. **The turn.** What the feeling tracks is fluency, not correctness. A spell guarantees effect through correct incantation; a prompt does not. The more convincing the output, the more the spell-feeling fires, so the feeling peaks exactly where the risk does.
4. **The mechanism.** The feeling is a signal to validate, not to trust. It marks the absence of ground truth, not its arrival. Rejoins the validation / ground-truth argument here without using the capitalised labels (writing-guide section 6).
5. **The landing.** The craft of prompting is real, but it is the craft of getting good output to appear, which is a different skill from knowing it is right. Soft forward look or open question. One CTA.

## Watch (from the editorial read)

- **Whimsy risk.** Section-2 reader (pragmatic, hype-tired senior engineer) bounces on "magic" framing fast. The metaphor has to pay off in a hard claim or it reads as another cute AI-takes piece. Keep the spell frame subordinate to the validation claim, not decorative.
- **Cliche risk.** "Prompt engineering as wizardry" is a worn LinkedIn trope. The novel move is "the spell-feeling is the tell that validation got skipped," not "prompting is like casting spells." Do not drift back into the trope.
- **Arc position.** This is a sibling, not an arc post. Do not let a strong draft here quietly displace the deferred asymmetry-frame closer. Run the arc-position audit (writing-guide section 9) before treating it as anything other than a standalone.

## Adjacent angle parked (not this essay)
